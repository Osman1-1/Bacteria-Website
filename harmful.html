Harmful Bacteria

WARNING: This page contains medical information if you are sensitive to such content please exercise caution.

Not all bacteria are beneficial. Some bacteria can cause infections and diseases in humans, animals, and plants. These bacteria are known as harmful or pathogenic bacteria. When harmful bacteria enter the body, they can multiply quickly and damage tissues or release toxins that make people sick.

Harmful bacteria can spread in many different ways. They may spread through contaminated food and water, through the air when people cough or sneeze, or through direct contact with infected surfaces or people. Poor hygiene and unsafe food preparation can increase the risk of bacterial infections.

Examples of Harmful Bacteria

  • Salmonella – can cause food poisoning, usually from contaminated food.
  • Escherichia coli (E. coli) – some types can cause severe stomach infections.
  • Streptococcus – can cause infections such as strep throat.
  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis – causes tuberculosis, a serious lung disease.

Symptoms of Bacterial Infections

Bacterial infections can cause a variety of symptoms depending on the type of bacteria and where the infection occurs in the body. Common symptoms may include fever, stomach pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, coughing, and inflammation. Some infections are mild, while others can be more serious if not treated promptly.

Treatment

Many bacterial infections can be treated using antibiotics, which are medicines that kill bacteria or stop them from growing. However, doctors must use antibiotics carefully because some bacteria can develop resistance to these medicines over time.
