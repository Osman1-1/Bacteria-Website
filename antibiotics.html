Antibiotics and Resistance

Antibiotics are medicines that are used to treat bacterial infections. They work by killing bacteria or stopping them from growing and reproducing. Antibiotics have been very important in modern medicine because they help treat many serious infections that were once very dangerous.

Doctors prescribe antibiotics to treat infections caused by bacteria, such as pneumonia, strep throat, and some types of food poisoning. However, antibiotics do not work against viruses like the common cold or flu.

Antibiotic Resistance

Antibiotic resistance happens when bacteria change in a way that makes antibiotics less effective. This means the medicine may no longer be able to kill the bacteria or stop it from growing. As a result, infections can become harder to treat.

Causes of Antibiotic Resistance

  • Overuse of antibiotics
  • Not finishing a full course of antibiotics
  • Using antibiotics when they are not needed

Why It Is a Problem

Antibiotic resistance is a growing problem around the world. If bacteria continue to become resistant to antibiotics, it may become more difficult to treat infections in the future. Scientists and doctors are working to find new medicines and encourage people to use antibiotics responsibly.

Always finish the full course of antibiotics unless your doctor tells you to stop.
